Film gives you twelve or thirty-six decisions per roll and no metadata to explain them. A year after the scan, the photograph is still beautiful, but the reason you stopped walking is gone. Field Notes is my attempt to keep the reason.

## One sentence, no more

The first version had fields: location, camera, lens, film stock, aperture. Beta testers told me it felt like homework, and they were right. So the app now asks for exactly one thing: **a sentence about why you took this one.** Everything else is captured passively — place, time, light — and everything else is optional.

That single constraint changed the feeling of the product. Adding a frame became a ten-second ritual instead of a form. The average note is fourteen words, which is roughly the length of a thought you can still trust an hour later.

## The contact sheet

The home screen is a contact sheet, not a feed. Four frames across, generous margins, a tiny sans-serif caption under each. Numbers are tabular and quiet; there are no likes, no counts, no red. Rolling the sheet feels like laying prints on a table, and a long press turns the whole sheet into a shareable image that looks like it came from a darkroom.

## What shipping alone taught me

Working without a team forces clarity about scope. I cut dark mode, then brought it back, then cut it again, and only shipped it when it cost me nothing to maintain. I kept the local database as the source of truth so the app works on a train and syncs when it can.

The most useful beta feedback was not about features. It was about the tone of the empty state — “No frames yet” read as a reprimand, so it became “Nothing yet. Go for a walk.”

## Next

Roll-level metadata from the camera app, a printable zine export, and an Android version if the next hundred testers still keep their notes. The app is small on purpose. It is the only way a side project survives a full-time job.
