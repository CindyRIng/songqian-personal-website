Recruiting data is only half useful. A list of job postings tells you a company is hiring; it does not tell you whether the company is real, how big it is, or who runs it. The useful chain is: keyword plus city → jobs → deduplicated companies → business-registration details → maybe more jobs from the same company. Doing that by hand in a browser is data entry; doing it with scripts requires a session that does not break.

zhilian-client is the second client in a small family, and I built it as a deliberate copy of the first. It turns Zhaopin's web product into a local read-only service with 17 operations exposed four ways: CLI, REST (loopback plus bearer, OpenAPI and self-contained docs), MCP stdio with 17 tools, and a SKILL workflow.

## The chain is the product

The operations follow the research loop, not the website: search jobs by keyword and city; page through a query by `queryId`; fetch job detail; list deduplicated companies from a query; fetch company detail including business registration; fetch all open jobs for a company. That last one is paginated upstream and easy to truncate — the client fetches all of them (21 of 21 in a real test) instead of quietly returning page one.

## Reuse as a strategy

After boss-client I had a shape that worked: single owner process, cloak browser session, an adapter layer for page-internal fetch and DOM extraction, zod domain models, generators for four contracts, a SQLite cache-aside with TTLs, a systemd unit. Building the second client was mostly substitution, not invention. That was the point: architecture you cannot reuse is architecture you cannot trust.

## Caching and the anti-bot reality

Search and company detail are cached from two hours to seven days with forced refresh, storing structured fields only — no HTML, no cookies, no captcha artifacts. Session tokens renew themselves (access ≈ 7 days, refresh ≈ 30 days). EdgeOne's human verification is handled with a relay: the service either clicks automatically or returns a screenshot with coordinates for a human or an agent to resolve.

## What I learned

The second implementation is where a design gets tested. Because the first client already defined the seams, I could add a different upstream, different pagination and a different anti-bot story without touching the architecture. 35 unit tests and generated contracts keep the four entry points honest. Read-only and minimal data collection are not just compliance notes; they are what make the tool boring enough to run unattended.
